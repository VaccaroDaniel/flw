# Adventure English World V2 - Unit 4 Difficulty Benchmark

Unit: 4 - Classroom Magic  
Target level: Pre-A1.1  
Primary age identity: young child learners, about 7-8 years old  
Purpose: protect the language difficulty before producing images, audio, quizzes, and HTML.

## Reference Direction

This unit is aligned with the common pattern in major young-learner English resources:

- Cambridge Young Learners / Pre A1 Starters: familiar picture-based tasks, short spoken instructions, matching, pointing, colouring/object actions, and confidence-building responses.
- British Council LearnEnglish Kids: classroom actions and classroom language are taught through word games, flashcards, listen-and-watch material, and short activity cycles.
- Cambridge Fun for Starters style: bright, full-colour, activity-led work with exam-style matching and speaking confidence tasks.
- National Geographic Learning young-learner style: songs, rhythm, games, movement, visual meaning, and real communication before grammar explanation.

## Difficulty Lock

Unit 4 is not a grammar unit. It is a listen-and-do classroom command unit.

New language must stay within these command chunks:

- Listen.
- Look.
- Point.
- Say.
- Say it again.
- Stand up.
- Sit down.
- Open your book.
- Close your book.
- Read.
- Write.
- Stop.
- please

Allowed polite chunks:

- Listen, please.
- Look, please.
- Point, please.
- Stand up, please.
- Sit down, please.
- Open your book, please.
- Close your book, please.
- Read, please.
- Write, please.

Review language from Units 1-3:

- Hello. Hi. Goodbye.
- I'm Leo / Mia / Emma.
- character names: Leo, Mia, Emma, Toto
- numbers 1-5 only as action order support
- colours only as card recognition support

Blocked language:

- full imperative grammar explanation
- do/don't commands
- can/can't
- must/mustn't
- prepositions of place
- plurals explanation
- family, age, countries, nationalities, geography
- animals and likes/dislikes from Unit 5
- long self-introductions

## Unit Content Standard

Each lesson must include:

1. A large image or scene that makes the meaning clear before text.
2. A short whole-listening model, not sentence-by-sentence overload.
3. A Study First panel with 3-5 command chunks.
4. One guided noticing point: "command = action."
5. Auto-check quizzes with mixed types.
6. A short output step: point, act, choose, repeat, copy, or record.
7. A repair path when the learner gets an answer wrong.

## Lesson Difficulty Growth

Lesson 1: hear and choose Listen / Look / Say.  
Lesson 2: follow Listen / Look / Point in a three-step order.  
Lesson 3: repeat Say / Say it again / One more time.  
Lesson 4: perform Stand up / Sit down.  
Lesson 5: connect Open your book / Close your book to object actions.  
Lesson 6: distinguish Read / Write and copy two command labels.  
Lesson 7: choose and rehearse four commands as a routine.  
Watch: understand the command routine in a mini video.  
Project: record four classroom action commands with "please."

## Quiz Difficulty Rules

Use short, varied quiz types:

- listen and choose the picture
- match command to icon
- match icon to command
- choose the next action
- reorder 3-4 commands
- true/false image check
- phrase completion with one missing word
- project readiness checklist

The unit should reach about 108 auto-checkable items, but item count must not make the page feel like a test-only page. The learning route is: image -> listening -> study -> guided quiz -> action output.

## Visual Requirements For Image Generation

Images must help learners understand classroom action meaning without relying on text.

Required visual clarity:

- Listen: ear or listening gesture.
- Look: eyes or looking gesture.
- Point: clear pointing finger.
- Say: speech bubble without readable text.
- Stand up: child standing beside chair.
- Sit down: child seated safely.
- Open your book: book visibly opening.
- Close your book: book visibly closed or closing.
- Read: eyes on card/book.
- Write: pencil on card/book.

Do not include grammar charts, dense writing, flags, country symbols, random text, or older learner/adult coursebook imagery.
